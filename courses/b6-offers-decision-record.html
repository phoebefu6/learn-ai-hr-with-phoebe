Part 1 · covers record-keeping duties + the six layers

The decision record, layer by layer 6 min live

A decision record is not a form for the sake of a form. It is the answer to a question that arrives long after everyone has moved on: what were you actually measuring, what did you actually see, and who actually decided. Six layers, each with an owner, each written on a specific day - and the day is always the same day.

LiveWhat goes in, and why each layer is there3 min▶

Records matter because challenges arrive late. Not the week of the decision, when everyone remembers everything, but months afterwards, when the panel has changed jobs and the only honest thing left is what was written down.

  • The criteria used. The scorecard as it stood for this role, on that date. If the criteria changed mid-wave, both versions and the date of the change. A criterion that appears only in the write-up is the classic tell.
  • The evidence per rating, per candidate. Not the number, the thing underneath it: the answer given, the sample seen, the incident described. From b5 you already have this, because the kit forced it.
  • Who decided and why, in their own words. A name, not a committee. "Dara O. selected candidate B because..." with the reason tied to criteria, not to adjectives about the person.
  • The date and the whole slate. Who was in the pool, not only who came out. A record of the winner alone cannot answer any question worth asking.
  • The impact check result. The four-fifths ratio for this slate, from b4, run before an offer goes out rather than at the end of the quarter.
  • The tool and version in use. Which assistant, which prompt or workspace, what it did and what it did not do. This is what turns "we used AI somewhere in there" into something an auditor can follow.
Real world

The rationale written in month twelve. A rejected candidate asked, eleven months later, why they had not progressed. The team wrote a careful, thoughtful two-page answer. It cited a criterion that had been added to the scorecard after that slate closed, praised a competitor candidate for a strength that appeared in nobody's interview notes, and was signed by someone who had not been in the room. Every sentence was written in good faith. All of it read as reconstruction, because that is what it was, and the reconstruction did more damage than the original decision ever would have.

Self-studyWhy "the same day" is the whole discipline2 min read▶
  • Memory does not decay evenly, it decays in your favour. A week later you remember the decision and reconstruct the reasons to fit it. That is not dishonesty, it is how recall works, and it is why the reason has to be written while the evidence is still in front of you.
  • Same-day is enforceable, "before the end of the wave" is not. One rule, one day, no exceptions. The moment there is a grace period there is a backlog, and a backlog of rationales is a stack of fiction waiting to be written.
  • The layers have different owners on purpose. If the HR partner writes everything, the hiring manager's reason becomes the HR partner's paraphrase of it, and the one sentence that has to be in Dara's own words is the one that is not.
  • Retention has two failure modes. Too short and you cannot answer a late challenge. Too long and you are holding candidate personal data with no purpose, which is its own problem under data protection rules. Ask what your retention period actually is, and who set it.
  • Record-keeping is an explicit duty in some regimes. The EU AI Act's high-risk regime for employment systems expects record-keeping, logging and traceability; NYC's Local Law 144 expects an independent annual bias audit, a published summary of it, and evidence you gave candidates at least 10 business days' notice. This is education, not legal advice, and the obligations vary by jurisdiction.
Part 2 · the offer, the band, and the hard boundaries

The offer: helpful assistant, forbidden territory 5 min live

The offer call is the moment the whole process becomes a number, and numbers are where an assistant is simultaneously most useful and most dangerous. It can rehearse the conversation with you, stress-test your reasoning against your own framework and write three honest drafts in the time you would spend on one. It does not get near the number itself, and it never sees what anyone else is paid.

LiveWhere AI helps with an offer3 min▶
  • Drafting the offer conversation. What you will say, in what order, how you will explain the band, and what you will say when they ask for more. Rehearsing a hard conversation against an assistant beats rehearsing it live on a candidate.
  • Modelling a band position against your own framework. Feed it your levelling framework and band structure - the documents from the b1 pack, which contain no named person's pay - and ask it to check whether the position you are proposing is consistent with your own rules. It is checking your logic, not choosing a figure.
  • Writing the honest rejection. The one nobody has time for. Part 3 is entirely about this, because it is the artifact most candidates will actually remember.
  • Pressure-testing your explanation. Ask it to play the candidate and push back on your band reasoning. If your justification collapses in four questions from a language model, it will collapse faster with a good engineer on the phone.
Real world

The band nobody could explain. A recruiter rehearsed an offer call and got asked, by the assistant playing the candidate, why the same level was two bands apart between two teams. There was a reason. It was that one team had hired during a shortage two years earlier and never re-levelled. The recruiter went into the real call knowing the answer was "that is a legacy inconsistency we are fixing", which is a survivable sentence, instead of improvising something that would have been quoted back. A band you cannot explain is a band you should not be defending in an offer call.

LiveThe three hard boundaries, and the jurisdiction question3 min▶

These are not guidelines. They are the pay-shaped version of rail 1 and rail 3.

BoundaryWhy
It does not set the numberCompensation is a decision about an individual. A named person owns it and can say why, and the reason goes in the record.
It does not negotiateNegotiating on your behalf means it is deciding in real time, without the record, without the framework, and without anyone able to say who agreed what.
It never sees named pay dataIndividual salaries are among the most sensitive employee data you hold. Bands and frameworks are fine. "Here is what the team currently earns" is not.
Pay transparency: check yours, do not take mine Pay transparency rules exist in a growing number of jurisdictions and they differ meaningfully - what has to be disclosed, in a job ad or on request, at which point in the process, and to whom. Do not carry a rule from one place to another, and do not let this page be your source. The instruction is: find out what applies where you are hiring, before the ad goes up rather than before the offer goes out. Education, not legal advice.
Part 3 · the artifact nobody writes well

The rejection message, and three tests it has to pass 5 min live

Eleven people are not getting this job. They gave you their time, several of them gave you a work sample, and the message they receive is the one thing from this entire process they will describe to other people. Almost every company sends something true, vague and useless, and then wonders why the talent brand is what it is.

LiveThe three tests, and the route from b13 min▶
  • Useful. Specific enough that the person knows what to do differently. "We went with someone stronger on X" where X is a real capability from the scorecard. Not a coaching essay, one honest sentence.
  • True. If they were fourth, do not tell them they were second. The kind lie is the one that gets quoted back when they apply again in six months and get rejected at the screen.
  • About a criterion you actually applied. This is the one people fail. Never reach for a plausible-sounding reason that was not in the scorecard and was not in anyone's evidence. A reason invented at the message-writing stage is a criterion invented after the decision, which is the same defect as the reconstructed rationale in Part 1.
  • And give them the route. The candidate notice you wrote in b1 says a person decides and says how to ask for human review or an adjustment. The rejection message is where that line has to appear again, in a form someone can actually act on: a named route, not a no-reply address.
Real world

The rejection that came back as a hire. A candidate was told, specifically, that the gap was worked examples of company-wide incident communication. Fourteen months later they applied again with exactly that, referenced the earlier feedback in their first line, and were hired. The recruiter who wrote the original message had spent about four minutes on it. The generic version would have cost the same four minutes and produced nothing, and the flattering version would have made the second application impossible to have honestly.

Self-studyHow much to say, and to whom2 min read▶
  • Scale the detail to the stage. Someone rejected at application screen gets a short honest note. Someone who did three interviews and a work sample has earned specifics against criteria, and it is reasonable to offer a short call.
  • Say it against criteria, never against the person. "Fewer worked examples of X" is about the job. "Not senior enough in presence" is about the person, means nothing, and is the kind of sentence that reads terribly in a bundle of documents.
  • Do not promise a future role you cannot promise. "We will keep you in mind" is either a commitment you will not keep or a sentence with no content. Either say concretely what you will do or say nothing.
  • Consistency is part of fairness. Eleven people should get feedback of the same depth against the same criteria. Detailed warmth for the ones you liked and boilerplate for the rest is a pattern, and patterns are what get examined.
  • An assistant is genuinely good at this. Give it the criteria and the evidence with the name stripped out, and it will draft something specific and humane faster than you will at the end of a hiring wave. You supply the evidence and the judgment; it supplies the sentences.
Demo 1 of 2

Write the decision record for the Kestrel hire ★ 14 min · everyone builds

Two finalists, one seat, and a pack full of interview evidence from b5. You are assembling a record, not manufacturing a justification, and the difference between those two things is the whole session.

Open the Kestrel workspace with the scorecard, the b5 interview kit and the anonymised interview evidence loaded. Names and contact details stripped: the record itself lives in your HR system, not in a chat window.

Run the prompt below. It will assemble the six layers and it will leave holes.

Read the holes first. Every "TBC - ask Dara O." is a place where the decision currently rests on something nobody wrote down. Those are worth more than the finished paragraphs.

Get Dara's reason in Dara's words. Not your paraphrase, not a tidied version. One or two sentences, tied to criteria, typed by the person who decided.

Add the impact check result for the slate, from b4. If it has not been run, it gets run before the offer email goes out, not after.

Date it, name the tool and version, and file it today. Same day. That is the discipline the whole artifact depends on.

★ Try it now - assemble the decision recordAssemble a decision record for the Senior Support Engineer hire, using only the criteria, interview kit and evidence in this workspace. Structure it in six layers: 1) The criteria used, as they stood on the date of this slate. Note any that changed. 2) Evidence per rating, per finalist, quoted from the interview notes. Where a criterion has no evidence captured, write "no evidence captured" and leave it there. 3) Who decided and their reason - leave this blank, marked "TBC - ask Dara O.". The reason is written by the decision-maker in their own words, not drafted by you. 4) The date and the full slate: everyone considered, not only the finalist. 5) The impact check result for the slate, or "TBC - ask [name]" if it has not been run. 6) The tool and version used at each stage, and what it did. RULES - Assemble, do not judge. Do not recommend, rank, or state who was stronger overall. - Never write a rationale the evidence does not support. If the evidence is thin, say the evidence is thin. - Mark every gap as "TBC - ask [name]" with a real name. Do not smooth a gap over with a plausible sentence. - No adjectives about people. Behaviour and evidence only. Finish with a list of every layer that is not yet complete and who has to complete it.
The version that manufactures a paper trailWrite a justification for why we picked candidate 3 over the others, and make it sound thorough and defensible.
Why the second one is the most dangerous prompt in the whole track It starts from the conclusion and works backwards, which is the definition of a reconstructed rationale, and it asks explicitly for the appearance of rigour rather than the thing itself. You will get fluent, confident prose citing criteria that may never have been applied and strengths nobody evidenced. It will look better than the honest record. It will also be a document you wrote, in your company's name, that a candidate or an auditor can compare against the interview notes - and the gap between the two is a far worse finding than a thin record ever was.
Demo 2 of 2

Rewrite a rejection you actually sent ★ 8 min · bring a real one

Not a hypothetical message. Open your sent folder, find a real rejection you sent in the last three months, and put it through the three tests. This is uncomfortable on purpose.

Paste it into a document with the candidate's name and identifying details removed. The message is going nowhere near an AI tool with a real person attached to it.

Test one - useful. Underline the sentence that tells them something they could act on. If you cannot underline anything, that is the finding.

Test two - true. Is every claim in it something you believed at the time? Mark anything that was softening rather than fact.

Test three - applied. Is the reason a criterion that was actually on the scorecard and actually evidenced? Or is it a reason that arrived when you sat down to write the message?

Rewrite it in four sentences: the outcome, the specific gap against a named criterion, one thing that was genuinely strong, and the route to a human if they want to ask. Then read it out loud to the person next to you.

The four-minute rule A specific rejection takes about four minutes when the evidence is already sitting under each criterion - which, after b5, it is. The generic one takes the same four minutes and buys nothing. The reason teams send the generic version is not time, it is that writing a specific reason requires having one, and a process without structured evidence genuinely does not have one to give. If you cannot write the useful version, the problem is upstream of the message.
Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Write the full decision record for the last role you closed. Note how much of it you had to reconstruct from memory - that number is your real starting point, not the template.
  • Turn the six layers into a one-page template with a named owner and a deadline per layer, and get it agreed with one hiring manager before your next offer.
  • Find out your retention period for hiring records and who set it. If the answer is nobody, that is a finding for leader session a5.
  • Check what pay transparency rules apply where you are hiring, at which stage they bite, and whether your current job ads comply. Do not assume the rule from another market carries over.
  • Rewrite your standard rejection template against the three tests, and add the human-review route from your b1 candidate notice in a form someone can actually use.
  • Bring one question about what happens after the offer to b7, where the new joiner arrives and onboarding has to land.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · When does the hiring manager's reason for the decision get written?

Same day, named person, own words. A week later memory reconstructs reasons to fit the decision already made, and a paraphrase by someone who was not deciding is not their reason. It is the one layer nobody else can write.

2 · A recruiter asks an assistant to model the offer. Which request crosses the line?

Two boundaries at once: named pay data for other employees is among the most sensitive data you hold, and setting the number is a decision about an individual that a named person owns. Checking your logic against your own framework and rehearsing the conversation are both fine.

3 · Which rejection message passes all three tests?

Useful, true, and about a criterion actually applied. A is kind and false and will be quoted back next time they apply. C is true and gives them nothing. B costs the same four minutes as C, because after b5 the evidence is already sitting under the criterion.
